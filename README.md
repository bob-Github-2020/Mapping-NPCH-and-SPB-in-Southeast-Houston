# Mapping-NPCH-and-SPB-in-Southeast-Houston


# Abstract

The New Pre-Consolidation Head (NPCH), Safe Pumping Buffer (SPB), and Safe Pumping Volume (SPV) are critical thresholds for the safe use of groundwater resources without reactivating land subsidence in post-subsidence urban areas. NPCH refers to the hydraulic head in a confined aquifer system at the time subsidence ceased; as long as heads remain above this level, permanent subsidence will not resume. SPB represents the difference between the current hydraulic head and the NPCH, indicating the maximum permissible hydraulic head decline before subsidence is reactivated. SPV estimates the maximum volume of groundwater that can be safely extracted within the SPB limit. Although these concepts are well-established theoretically, their practical application is hindered by the complex temporal and spatial variability of hydraulic heads in thick confined aquifer systems, as well as limitations in field monitoring data such as the spatial density and temporal coverage of monitoring wells. This study demonstrates practical approaches for mapping NPCH, SPB, and SPV in Harris-Galveston Subsidence District (HGSD) of southeast Houston, Texas. The resulting maps reveal significant spatial variability in subsidence vulnerability and available safe reserves. Critically, the NASA-Clear Lake area—a densely populated high-technology hub hosting the NASA Johnson Space Center—retains only approximately 5 m of SPB as of 2025, implying that increased groundwater pumping in this area could promptly reinitiate unrecoverable subsidence. In contrast, the adjacent Pasadena area maintains a substantially larger SPB of 24 m, with a corresponding SPV sufficient to serve as an emergency water source for neighboring zones. As of 2025, the total SPV across HGSD Areas 1 and 2 is estimated at about 58 MGD (million gallons per day) on an annual basis, equivalent to roughly 1.5 times recent annual groundwater use. The mapping techniques for NPCH, SPB, and SPV presented here are adaptable and can be implemented in other urban areas where subsidence has ceased.
